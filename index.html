Recommended a centralized component decision matrix: prioritized dual-sourcing on high-cost silicon and passive components, and surfaced documentation exceptions.

08 / IMPLEMENTATION

Structured standardized repository in SharePoint and Excel; coordinated with engineering to resolve drawing discrepancies and update Arena PLM change orders.

09 / RESULT

Identified and validated a $1.2M annualized sourcing opportunity, presented to sourcing leadership to anchor upcoming supplier negotiations.

10 / HOW MEASURED

Calculated total landed price delta between baseline quotes and benchmark supplier price curves at scaled annual volume forecasts.

11 / WHAT I LEARNED

01 / PROBLEM

Picker transit times were inflated due to random item placement, leading to congestion in picking aisles and inventory audit discrepancies across 1,000+ SKUs.

02 / WHY IT MATTERED

During campus peak order rushes, fulfillment delays cascaded into retail stockouts and overtime labor expenses.

03 / BASELINE

8.4 km representative travel per worker shift batch; cycle counting accuracy fluctuated below 90% in high-velocity bays.

04 / CONSTRAINTS

Fixed facility layout; zero budget for conveyor automation; layout changes could not interrupt ongoing store dispatches.

05 / DATA

06 / ANALYSIS

Pareto 80/20 analysis revealed that 18% of SKUs accounted for 79% of all picker touches, yet were located throughout distant aisles.

07 / MY RECOMMENDATION

Re-slot Zone A fast-movers adjacent to dispatch staging; establish dedicated replenishment lanes and visual bin locator barcodes.

08 / IMPLEMENTATION

Executed staged slotting migration during off-peak weekend windows; built Excel exception trackers to audit bin transfers.

09 / RESULT

Modeled picker travel reduced by 15.5% (to 7.1 km); restocking cycle time reduced by 15%; inventory record accuracy sustained above 98%.

10 / HOW MEASURED

GPS simulated pick-path route calculations benchmarked against daily cycle-count audit logs.

11 / WHAT I LEARNED

Warehouse efficiency is fundamentally a geometry and velocity problem. Clean slotting delivers higher ROI than complex software overlays.

12 / WHAT I WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY

I would implement dynamic seasonal re-slotting triggers into the inventory management system rather than running quarterly reviews.

ACADEMIC / RESEARCH INVENTORY OPTIMIZATION & STOCHASTIC MODELING

Multi-Echelon Inventory Optimization & Safety Stock Modeling

USC Viterbi Optimization Research · Evaluating Working Capital vs. Service Level

01 / PROBLEM

Multi-echelon distribution networks face conflicting incentives: holding inventory near consumers improves fill rates but inflates working capital, while centralized holding introduces lead-time stockout risks.

02 / WHY IT MATTERED

Uncoordinated safety stock policies across regional distribution centers create the bullwhip effect and trap millions in tied-up operating capital.

03 / BASELINE

Decentralized safety stock buffers calculated independently at each warehouse node with static lead-time assumptions.

04 / CONSTRAINTS

Non-stationary customer demand; lead-time variance; minimum order quantity (MOQ) hurdles; strict 95% target service levels.

05 / DATA

Simulated multi-year demand series, supplier lead-time distributions, carrying cost percentages, and stockout penalty costs.

06 / ANALYSIS

Formulated non-linear optimization models in Python (SciPy) and Excel Solver evaluating trade-offs between holding costs and stockout risks.

07 / MY RECOMMENDATION

Implement guaranteed-service model (GSM) safety-stock bounds, pooling high-variance safety stock at central hubs while maintaining lean forward buffers.

08 / IMPLEMENTATION

Constructed a sensitivity dashboard allowing inventory planners to dynamically model service-level trade-offs under lead-time spikes.

09 / RESULT

Modeled an 8% to 15% reduction in total inventory holding costs across regional distribution centers while sustaining target fill rates above 95%.

10 / HOW MEASURED

Monte Carlo simulation runs comparing stochastic stockout frequencies and total inventory carrying cost over 52 simulated weeks.

11 / WHAT I LEARNED

Safety stock should not be spread evenly across an enterprise. Risk pooling at upstream nodes offers superior resilience at lower capital cost.

12 / WHAT I WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY

I would incorporate dynamic freight rate tariffs into the objective function to evaluate the interaction between inventory holding and expedited shipping costs.
